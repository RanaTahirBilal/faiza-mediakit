Modest Beauty on Instagram in New York: Why This Segment Is Growing Fast in 2026

By Faiza Nadeem · Published September 08, 2026 · Long Island, New York

Modest beauty content on Instagram has grown into one of the platform's most under-served, high-engagement niches. The audience is real, growing, and largely untapped by mainstream beauty brands. If you are a brand marketer or a creator watching this space, here is what actually matters in 2026.

The size of the modest beauty audience

Modest beauty content covers makeup, skincare, and fashion styling for women who dress modestly, including hijabi creators, conservative Christian creators, orthodox Jewish creators, and any woman who prefers less skin-exposure content. The audience for this content on Instagram alone is estimated in the tens of millions globally.

For my content specifically, based on Long Island, my monthly unique viewer count sits at 3.06 million. The engagement rate per view is 11.5 percent, which is roughly 5 to 10 times the beauty category average on Instagram Reels.

Why modest beauty content over-performs

Three things drive the outsized engagement I see and that other modest beauty creators report:

Underserved audience

Mainstream beauty content typically shows creators with visible hair and skin exposure. Modest beauty audiences have historically had to work harder to find content that reflects how they actually get dressed and made up. When they find a creator who does, they engage deeply.

Aesthetic distinction

Modest beauty content has a distinct visual grammar. Head coverings, higher necklines, long sleeves, and specific styling choices create instantly recognizable Reels. Instagram's algorithm reads visual patterns fast, and a distinct visual identity accelerates recommendation.

Cross-audience appeal

Modest beauty content is not just for modest audiences. Makeup tutorials, skincare reviews, and product demos have universal appeal. My page pulls a majority non-hijabi audience even though I wear the hijab. The content is about beauty. The styling is where the modesty lives.

Where mainstream beauty brands miss the opportunity

Most beauty PR programs are still built around a narrow creator profile: young, follower-count driven, aesthetic that matches the brand's existing campaigns. Modest beauty creators are often filtered out early because they do not match the visual mold.

The brands that recognize this and lean in early get outsized returns. Estee Lauder has been notably ahead through their Community x Seen program, which is where I partner with them. Dr. Ceuracle and ISNTREE, both K-beauty skincare brands, work with me on skincare-focused content that does not require any specific styling constraint.

What brands should look for in a modest beauty creator

If you are a beauty or skincare brand exploring this segment, three signals matter more than follower count:

  1. Engagement rate per view above 5 percent. High engagement per view signals content the algorithm is happy to keep serving.
  2. Discovery reach percentage above 70 percent. This means the creator's audience is mostly cold viewers, not just their follower base, which extends your product's exposure.
  3. Content-brand fit beyond styling. A creator whose product reviews feel like they could sit inside your brand campaign is more valuable than one whose follower count is high but whose voice does not match yours.

The modest beauty scene on Long Island

Long Island and the broader New York area have quietly become a hub for modest beauty content. The New York South Asian and Muslim communities are large enough to support a real creator ecosystem, and the region's access to major cosmetics events, launches, and brand offices makes it easy to build brand relationships from here.

If you are a brand looking to seed products with Long Island modest beauty creators or run a campaign in this segment, the entry point is simple: identify creators with strong monthly reach and engagement rate, and build the partnership around content that lives inside their existing aesthetic rather than trying to bend them into a mainstream campaign shape.
