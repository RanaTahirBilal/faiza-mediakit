How to Land Your First Beauty Brand Partnership: A New York Creator's Playbook

By Faiza Nadeem · Published September 14, 2026 · Long Island, New York

Nobody's first brand deal comes from waiting to be discovered. Every partnership I have signed with Estée Lauder, Dr. Ceuracle, and ISNTREE started with me sending a specific pitch to a specific person at the brand. Below is the actual method, not the influencer-course version.

Before you pitch anything, get your setup honest

Brand partnership teams look at three things in the first thirty seconds of your profile:

  • Do your last five posts look like content they could stand next to their own campaign?
  • Is your bio clear about what you do and how to reach you?
  • Do you have visible reach (in views or engagement), even if follower count is small?

If your last five posts include a cousin's engagement party photo and a food picture, that is your first fix. Not because those posts are bad, but because they make the brand's job harder. They have to imagine your grid without those posts. Most will not.

Your bio needs three things: what kind of content you make, the city or region you are in (this matters for brand campaigns), and a business email address. Not your personal Gmail. Get a domain-based email if you can. A creator with partnerships@yourname.com reads as ready to work.

Who to pitch, and how to find them

The person you want to reach is the influencer coordinator, community manager, or partnerships associate at the brand. Not the CEO. Not the founder's personal Instagram.

How to find them, for free:

  • LinkedIn search: "brand's name" + "influencer" or "community" or "partnerships"
  • The brand's own press page or careers page (job listings tell you the org chart)
  • Muck Rack for beauty PR contacts, if your budget allows a free trial

Send your pitch to the person, not the general email. General emails go nowhere. Personal emails get read.

The pitch that works (and the one that does not)

The pitch that does not work: "Hi! I would love to work with your brand. My email is X."

The pitch that works, in one email, under 150 words:

  1. The first line names the person and one specific thing about the brand's recent activity (a launch, a campaign, a shift). This shows you paid attention.
  2. Second paragraph is one line about you and one concrete number. "@yourhandle. Beauty creator in New York. 200K monthly reach on Instagram Reels."
  3. Third paragraph is what you are proposing. Not "let's collaborate." Something specific: "One Reel featuring [product] with organic tie-in to my existing content style."
  4. Fourth paragraph is a link to your media kit or your best three Reels.
  5. Last line: your rate, or "open to standard rates" if you do not have one yet.

Send from your business email. Attach nothing. Pitches with attachments get flagged.

What to charge before you have a rate card

The awkward part. Nobody tells new creators what to charge, so most either undersell by half or overshoot and lose the deal.

A rough starting benchmark for a New York-based beauty creator with under 10K followers but real reach (100K+ monthly views):

  • One in-feed Instagram Reel with brand tag: $300 to $800
  • One Reel plus one Story frame: $500 to $1,200
  • UGC deliverable (video sent to brand for their own use, not posted to your feed): $200 to $500 per asset

These are starting points, not ceilings. Once you have three deals under your belt, raise your rate 50 percent. Once you have ten, double it.

Never work for free product only, unless the product is something you would have bought yourself and the brand is a genuine step up in your positioning (a first Estée Lauder gifted send when you have never worked with a big beauty house before, for example).

What to negotiate that is not the money

New creators focus entirely on the rate. Experienced creators negotiate three other things every time:

  • Usage rights. Standard deal gives the brand rights to repost your content on their own channels for a set time (30 to 90 days). Longer or wider usage means higher fee. Never give perpetual usage on a first deal.
  • Whitelisting. If the brand wants to run your content as a paid ad from your handle, that is a separate line item. Do not include it in the base rate.
  • Exclusivity. If the brand asks you not to work with competitors for a period, that costs them. A 30-day category exclusivity clause is standard. 90 days is a discount to them. 6 months is a full-year salary.

What to expect after the first deal

Most brand partnerships teams do not respond to the first pitch. This is normal. The response rate for cold beauty brand pitches is around 8 to 12 percent. Send twenty pitches, expect two replies, land one deal.

Once you have your first deal delivered on time and on brief, ask for a testimonial or a case study reference. This turns your one deal into leverage for the next five.

Your second deal usually pays 20 to 40 percent more than your first. This is because the brand knows you can deliver. Do not undersell yourself out of gratitude for the first opportunity.

The unglamorous part nobody talks about

Landing brand deals is 80 percent operations. Following up on invoices, tracking usage windows, sending timely content, replying to briefs within 48 hours. Creators who lose deals in the second round usually lose them for missing a deadline, not for a bad Reel.

Build a simple spreadsheet. Track: brand name, contact, date pitched, date responded, rate agreed, deliverables due, deliverables sent, invoice sent, invoice paid. This alone puts you ahead of most creators in your reach bracket.
