How to Get 1 Million Views on Instagram Reels: A Creator's Playbook

By Faiza Nadeem · Published September 14, 2026 · Long Island, New York

My Instagram page has 4,046 followers. Last month it pulled 3.06 million unique viewers. That is a 750-to-1 ratio between viewers and followers, and it is not luck or a viral fluke. It is the result of a specific way of making Reels that works with the algorithm, not against it.

Below is the honest version of how I do it. Not "post at 8 PM Tuesday" advice. The real levers.

Understand what the algorithm is actually optimizing for

Instagram's Reels algorithm is not measuring likes. It is measuring how much of the video people watch, whether they re-watch, whether they save, and whether they share.

Every Reel gets a small first push to a test audience of a few hundred people. If the retention data from that push is strong, the algorithm pushes to more people. Weak retention, the Reel dies at a few thousand views. Strong retention, it can run for weeks.

The whole game is retention. Everything below is downstream of that.

The first 1.5 seconds decide everything

Most viewers who scroll past your Reel do it in the first second. If your opening frame is confusing, low-contrast, or looks like every other beauty Reel, you lose them before they even see what you are doing.

What works:

  • Direct eye contact in frame 1
  • An unfinished action mid-motion, so viewers watch to see the result
  • A specific hook in the first two words: "This product...", "Nobody talks about...", "I stopped..."
  • A visual contrast (before/after halves, an object being applied) that promises payoff

What kills a Reel:

  • A slow intro where you introduce yourself before the content starts
  • Background music that starts before the visual makes sense
  • Text overlay that takes longer than one second to read

Shoot for retention, not aesthetics

Beautiful Reels are not always high-retention Reels. Overly cinematic openings, slow pans, long transitions, they look great but they cost you retention seconds you cannot afford.

My rule: 15-second Reels for the highest reach. If a topic needs more than 15 seconds, split it into two Reels.

Keep cuts fast. 1.5 to 2 seconds per scene. If a viewer's eye lands on nothing new for three seconds, they scroll.

Post cadence beats posting time

Whatever obsessive advice you have read about "the best time to post," ignore it. Discovery-algorithm Reels are not distributed by follower time zone. They are distributed by interest match, and the algorithm keeps pushing a good Reel for days.

What actually matters: post enough that the algorithm has data. My current cadence is 5 Reels per week. Below 3, the algorithm loses signal on you. Above 7, you stop being able to make each one land.

Repeat visual identity, not repeat content

The reason returning viewers recognize a creator in half a second is visual consistency. Same color palette, same framing, same energy in the opening frame.

You do not need to make the same video every time. You need viewers to know it is you the moment they see it. In beauty specifically, that means: same lighting setup, same backdrop, same style of intro shot. Not the same product, not the same look.

Hooks that work in the beauty niche

These are hook formats I have watched perform, both mine and other creators I follow:

  • "The [product category] I use every day and it is under $[price]"
  • "POV: You have 5 minutes to get ready for [context]"
  • "Nobody tells you this about [common product], but..."
  • "I stopped using [popular thing] and here is what I use instead"
  • "Testing [luxury brand] vs [drugstore brand]"

Notice what these have in common: they promise information in the first line. Not entertainment. Beauty viewers scroll to learn something they can use.

What does not work anymore

Long GRWM (get ready with me) style Reels where you talk through your whole day. They performed in 2023. In 2026 they die at 20K views.

Trending audio applied to unrelated content. The algorithm now flags this and demotes.

Repost from TikTok with the watermark visible. Instant demotion.

Overly polished, corporate-feeling content. Reads as ad. Viewers scroll.

The metric to actually track

Follower growth is a vanity metric for discovery-algorithm creators. My follower count moves slowly because most of my viewers do not follow. And that is fine.

The metric I check every Monday: monthly unique viewers. That is the number that tells me my content is landing with new people. It is also the number brand partners actually care about.

You cannot see monthly unique viewers on a per-Reel basis. You see it in Instagram Insights, Accounts Reached, past 30 days. That is your real growth signal.

The unfair advantage no one talks about

The creators who reach millions of viewers on small follower counts are not doing anything magical. They are doing this exact routine consistently for months. Most people quit at week six because they see slow follower growth. That is when the reach actually starts compounding.

If you are one month in and no Reel has broken 50K views, keep going. If you are six months in and still nothing, then reassess.
