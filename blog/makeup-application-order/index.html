The Correct Order to Apply Makeup, Explained by a New York Content Creator

By Faiza Nadeem · Published September 14, 2026 · Long Island, New York

This is the most-Googled makeup question of the last five years. Every tutorial has a slightly different answer. The reason: there is a right order, but it depends on the formula of each product, and nobody explains why.

Below is the order that works, and the actual reason behind each step. Once you understand the logic, you can adapt it to whatever products you own.

The one rule that solves 90 percent of confusion

Water-based products go on before oil-based or silicone-based products. Cream products go on before powder products. That is the whole rule.

Everything else in the order is a consequence of this one rule. Once you internalize it, you never have to memorize a step-by-step list again.

Step 1: Skincare, in this order

  1. Cleanser (rinse off)
  2. Toner or essence (thin water-based liquid)
  3. Serum (thicker water-based)
  4. Eye cream (targeted, thin cream)
  5. Moisturizer (cream)
  6. Sunscreen (last skincare step, always)

Why sunscreen last: SPF forms a film on the skin that protects from UV. If you put moisturizer on top of sunscreen, you break that film. Any brand that says "put moisturizer over SPF" is telling you to reduce your own sun protection.

Wait 60 to 90 seconds between skincare and makeup. Skincare needs to absorb before you touch it. Applying primer or foundation to still-wet moisturizer is what makes makeup pill up and roll off your face in tiny worms.

Step 2: Primer (only if you actually need it)

Primer is the most overhyped product in makeup. Most people do not need one. Primer helps if you have specific issues: very oily T-zone, very large pores, or if you are on camera under bright lights.

If you use primer, choose based on your actual problem:

  • Oily zones: a mattifying silicone-based primer, only on the T-zone, not the whole face
  • Dry patches: a hydrating water-based primer, only on the dry areas
  • Large pores: a blurring silicone primer, only where the pores are

Never one primer on the whole face. Your face has different needs in different zones.

Step 3: Color correction (if needed)

Color corrector goes on before concealer, because you are correcting the color of the underlying skin so the concealer above it can look neutral.

Peach or salmon under the eyes for blue or purple darkness. Green on redness (only if the redness is bright, not for general warmth). Yellow on any purple or grey areas.

A tiny amount, patted (not rubbed), and given 30 seconds to set before the next step.

Step 4: Foundation

Foundation goes after color correction and before concealer, because concealer should be applied only where foundation alone cannot cover.

Apply with fingers (warmest, blends the best), a damp beauty sponge (most even coverage), or a stippling brush (fastest for camera work). Not a flat foundation brush unless you want streaks.

Start in the center of the face and blend outward. The center of your face needs the most coverage. The edges need almost none.

Step 5: Concealer

Concealer after foundation. Reason: many spots and dark areas get 80 percent covered by foundation alone. Applying concealer first means you use more concealer than you need, and it always creases more.

Under-eye concealer goes in a triangle from the inner corner down to the top of the cheekbone. Not just under the lash line. The triangle is what makes it read as "brightened face" instead of "concealer on top of a shadow."

Step 6: Setting powder (only where oily)

Powder before cream blush is a mistake. Powder before cream blush makes the blush sit on top and look patchy.

Powder only the T-zone and only where you get oily. Do not powder the cheeks. Do not powder the temples. Keep the areas that will get cream blush and cream highlight powder-free.

If your product order is cream products first, powder locks them in place. If you use only powder blush and bronzer, then yes, powder goes before them.

Step 7: Cream blush, cream bronzer, cream highlight

All cream products go in this order: bronzer or contour first (to shape), blush second (for warmth), highlight third (for glow).

Blend each with a damp beauty sponge or a fluffy brush. Cream products blend into skin. They should not sit as three visible layers.

Step 8: Powder blush, bronzer, highlight (only if you skipped cream)

If you did cream products in step 7, you can skip powder color entirely, or use a tiny amount of powder blush over cream blush to lock the shape.

Do not layer heavy powder color over cream color. It reads as makeup on top of makeup, not as skin.

Step 9: Eyes

Eye makeup can happen at the very end (many pros do it first, before foundation, so any fallout gets cleaned up with the base). For a normal daily routine, doing eyes after face is faster.

Within the eye:

  1. Eyeshadow primer or a concealer swiped on the lid
  2. Transition color in the crease
  3. Lid color
  4. Liner
  5. Mascara

Step 10: Brows

Brows can go anytime. Many creators do them last so the finished face guides how much brow the look actually needs.

If your brows are naturally full, brow gel only. If they need filling, a pencil first in the sparse areas, then gel on top.

Step 11: Lips

Always last. Lip products transfer everywhere and get on brushes, on fingers, on other products. Save them for the end.

Lip liner first (light hand), lipstick or gloss second.

Step 12: Setting spray

Two mists, arm's length. Do not soak. A soaked face melts the makeup, not sets it.

Wait 30 seconds for it to dry before touching your face. This is when your makeup actually gels together and starts looking like skin, not layers.

What to do if you already applied out of order

You do not have to restart. If you put concealer before foundation and it looks fine, leave it. Most rules can be broken by a specific product working differently than expected.

The order matters most when things stop working: makeup that pills, blush that looks patchy, foundation that breaks up. That is when checking the order fixes it.
